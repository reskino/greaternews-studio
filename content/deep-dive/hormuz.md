# GreaterNews Deep Dive — The Strait of Hormuz

*Researched 2026-07-28. Fast-moving conflict story — treat live figures as "as of" their source
dates and re-check before publishing.*

**One line:** A narrow sea between Iran and Oman carries about a fifth of the world's oil — and
fighting there has pushed crude past $100 and pushed up the price at Ghana's pumps.

**Ghana hook (why we're covering a world story):** Ghana imports its refined fuel, priced in
dollars off the global market. When Hormuz flares, crude jumps, the cedi slips, and NPA price
windows rise — petrol went to about GH¢14.52/litre from July 16. This is the "world event → your
pocket" through-line the whole video hangs on.

**Confidence:** Core facts corroborated across 3+ independent outlets. The war/ceasefire status is
volatile — verify the "what's next" section on the day of publishing.

## Timeline (how we got here)
- **Q1 2026:** Oil through the strait already down to ~14.6 M barrels/day (from ~20.9 M in H1 2025) as
  conflict with Iran disrupted flows. [6][5]
- **April 2026:** Iran attacks Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline (a key bypass), cutting ~700,000
  barrels/day of alternative capacity. [6]
- **Week of July 20, 2026:** Iran attacks a tanker in the strait, forcing the crew to abandon ship;
  three commercial ships reported hit; Iran effectively closes the waterway. [4][2]
- **July 21, 2026:** U.S. carries out fresh strikes on Iran; a 10-day ceasefire reported "in focus." [2]
- **July 22, 2026:** Tehran responds; Trump warns of strikes on bridges/power plants over Hormuz
  attacks and "major military punishment" if Houthis hit ships again. [3]
- **July 23, 2026:** Oil tops $100/barrel (first time since May); benchmark up ~40% in three weeks;
  Houthis hit a Saudi ship in the Red Sea, threatening the Bab al-Mandeb bypass too. [1]

## The key facts (each source-tagged)
- The strait is the world's most important oil chokepoint — normally ~20% of global oil traffic /
  ~20.9 M barrels/day (H1 2025). [4][5]
- It is ~33 km (21 miles) wide at its narrowest, between Iran and Oman — widely reported. *(evergreen)*
- **"Very few alternative options exist to move oil out of the strait if it is closed"** — EIA. [5][6]
- Alternatives are thin: the Abqaiq–Yanbu NGL line (~300 kb/d) is already "fully utilised." [6]
- Over a quarter of global LNG trade also transits Hormuz — so gas is exposed too. [5]

## Why it matters (global + who's affected)
- A closed Hormuz has no real workaround, so prices spike fast and worldwide — this is why crude ran
  up ~40% in three weeks. [1][5]
- Asia (esp. China) is the biggest buyer of Hormuz oil, but the *price* shock is global — importers
  everywhere pay more. [5]

## The Ghana impact (the payoff)
- Petrol price floor raised to **GH¢13.28/litre** for the second July window (from GH¢12.79); average
  pump price projected to **~GH¢14.52/litre**, up **3.79%–5.31%**, effective **July 16, 2026**. [7][8][10]
- Diesel to **~GH¢16.00/litre** (up ~0.4%). [7]
- Drivers named: rising international refined-product prices + Hormuz shipping-security fears + a cedi
  that slipped ~0.56% over the window. [7][8]
- A Ghanaian outlet explicitly links Iran's Hormuz closure to the threat of further pump hikes. [11]

## What's next / open questions (verify on publish day)
- Does the reported 10-day ceasefire hold, or do strikes resume? [2][3]
- Do the Houthis blockade Bab al-Mandeb, closing the main bypass? [1]
- If crude stays above $100, expect further NPA window increases in Ghana. [1][7]
